MEASURING REAL-WORLD UTILITY

Pilot measurement protocol.

AirGuard is a decision-support prototype, not a demonstrated pollution-reduction intervention. Use this guide to assess usefulness without overstating impact.

1. Study design

Recruit consenting adult volunteers from one school or neighborhood group. Avoid recording health diagnoses or children’s personal information. Record the date, participant count and how participants used the planning tool. Participation must be voluntary.

2. Outcome measures

  • Comprehension: can participants distinguish regional modeled forecasts from ground sensor measurements?
  • Usability: can they identify a lower-modeled-PM2.5 time window within two minutes?
  • Intention: do they report intending to change timing? Keep this separate from observed behavior.
  • Reported action: after seven days, how many report acting on an AirGuard plan?
  • Community response: how many institutions were contacted, and which responded? Keep dated evidence.

3. Integrity and reporting

Report actual sample sizes, missing responses and any setbacks. Never claim AirGuard reduced pollutant concentration, prevented illness, or caused behavior change without evidence and an appropriate study design. Use aggregate counts only and omit identifying information from public reports.

4. Safety

Consult official local alerts, avoid recommendations that require risky outdoor exposure, do not collect sensitive medical information, and make clear that CAMS Global forecasts are not street-level readings.
